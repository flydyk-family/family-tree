How literal should the oak be?

All three use the same faded XIX-century palette and the same data. They differ in how the artwork relates to the genealogy — which drives build effort, scalability, and readability.

A · Painterly illustrated oak

A hand-illustrated oak (fixed artwork); member portrait-medallions are placed onto branches. Most beautiful & on-brand.

⚠ Layout is largely hand-authored — hard to auto-fit arbitrary numbers of people; fights the "edit mode" future goal.

B · Generative organic tree

Branches are computed from the genealogy — each family split is a fork, leaves are childless/unknown members. Organic curves + bark/leaf textures over the top.

✓ Adapts to any data & future edits. ✓ Still looks like an oak. ⚠ Most engineering in the layout algorithm.

C · Oak-styled structured chart

A clean generational node-link chart (like a classic family tree) skinned with bark connectors, leafy nodes, parchment & faded palette.

✓ Easiest to build & most readable at scale. ⚠ Reads as a "diagram in oak costume," less of a real oak.

My recommendation: B (generative organic tree) — it keeps the real oak feel you described, adapts to any family size, and naturally supports the future edit mode and zoom-to-cluster (a cluster is just a sub-branch).
